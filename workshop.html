Call for papers

The invitation

Share what you are learning

Generative artificial intelligence is rapidly transforming university teaching and learning. Students and instructors are experimenting with new tools for content generation, tutoring, feedback, assessment, and course design.

At the same time, universities face important questions concerning reliability, academic integrity, privacy, inclusion, sustainability, and the development of critical skills.

The PoliTO Community Workshop on Generative AI in University Teaching brings together the Politecnico di Torino community to share teaching experiences, methodologies, and open questions regarding the use of generative AI in higher education.

The workshop is organized by AI4Teach@PoliTO 2027, a TLLab group.

We welcome systematic research and experience-based contributions, including critical evaluations, unsuccessful experiments, and lessons learned.
Scope

Topics of interest

The list is indicative, not exhaustive. Interdisciplinary and discipline-specific perspectives are equally welcome.

  • Generative AI for course and learning material design
  • AI-supported tutoring, feedback, and student assistance
  • Uses of generative AI by students
  • Assessment redesign and academic integrity
  • Training and support for university instructors
  • Empirical evaluation of AI-supported teaching practices
  • Applications in engineering, architecture, and design
  • Personalization, accessibility, and inclusion
  • Bias, fairness, and responsible AI
  • Privacy, intellectual property, and data protection
  • Environmental and social sustainability
  • Student autonomy, creativity, and learning
  • Institutional policies and governance
  • Technical tools and learning-platform integration
  • Limitations, failures, and unintended consequences
Who can submit

For the whole PoliTO community

Collaborations involving different departments and professional backgrounds are especially encouraged.

Teaching & research

Faculty members, researchers, postdoctoral researchers, PhD candidates, and teaching assistants.

Professional expertise

Technical-administrative staff involved in teaching support, services, platforms, policy, and governance.

Student experience

Students examining the uses, opportunities, limitations, and effects of generative AI in their learning.

Contribution formats

Many ways to contribute

Whatever the format, papers should clearly describe their context, objectives, approach, and main findings or lessons learned.

Evidence & evaluation

Research studies, empirical evaluations, and careful analyses of teaching experiences.

Practice & design

Case studies, methods, tools, educational resources, and course or assessment redesigns.

Perspectives & initiatives

Position papers, institutional initiatives, policy discussions, and responsible adoption frameworks.

Ready to shape your contribution?

Start with a 300–500 word abstract registered through Microsoft Forms by 15 December 2026.
